# PictureFrames

*A play on words: "picture" (bring attention to) + "frames" (the perceptive capacity of drawing a boundary around something).*

---

## The Core Idea

We perceive reality through concepts. Every concept is a frame — a way of highlighting certain aspects of experience while backgrounding others. **PictureFrames** is the meta-awareness that this is happening, and the freedom that comes with it.

When you recognise that beliefs are frames rather than direct access to reality, you gain agency. You stop being *inside* the frame and start being the one holding it. Belief becomes a tool you wield rather than a cage you inhabit.

This is **meta-memetic capacity**: operating *on* the mechanism of idea-adoption itself, rather than just *within* it.

---

## The Reflective Loop

PictureFrames isn't just about choosing frames. It's a process:

1. **Noticing** — recognising you're in a frame
2. **Tracing** — understanding what that frame presupposes
3. **Evaluating** — checking coherence (does this frame contradict itself or its foundations?)
4. **Choosing** — with awareness, from clarity rather than default

The freedom lives here. Not in arbitrary belief-swapping, but in understanding structure well enough to make informed choices.

---

## The Hierarchy of Frames

Concepts aren't flat. They stack. Some frames presuppose others — you can't have "justice" without "harm," can't have "harm" without "self," can't have "self" without some form of boundary-drawing, continuity, perspective.

**Epistemic archaeology**: digging down to find what's presupposed. Candidate foundational frames include:
- **Boundary** — distinguishing this from that
- **Continuity** — something persists through change
- **Causation** — things bring about other things
- **Perspective** — there's a somewhere from which experience happens

These may be conditions of experience itself. But recognising them gives leverage. You can ask: *what would change if I held this differently?*

---

## Seeing Frames Requires Alternatives

You can't see a frame from inside it. Frames become visible only when alternatives appear. The water becomes visible when you glimpse air.

Example: The "hard problem of consciousness" is only hard within certain frames:
- **Dualism**: Mind and matter are separate → interaction problem
- **Physicalism**: Mind reduces to matter → hard problem
- **Idealism**: Matter reduces to mind → hard problem dissolves, different challenges arise
- **Panpsychism**: Consciousness is fundamental → combination problem

The default frame is *one option among many*. That's the PictureFrames move — not solving problems, but showing they're frame-dependent.

---

## Why Community Matters

You can't PictureFrame alone. Your blind spots are invisible *because* they're blind spots. You need other minds holding different frames to reveal what you can't see.

This is where [[Phenomenology Club]] becomes essential — not just for exploring experience, but for exposing each other to alternative frames. The diversity of the group expands everyone's frame-awareness.

Connection to broader human thought matters too. Philosophy, science, spirituality, culture — the larger reservoir of frames to draw from.

---

## The Articulation Trap

If there's something beneath all frames — some irreducible ground — framing it might be a category error. The ground might only be accessible obliquely, through practice or negative definition.

Maybe the project isn't to *find* the ultimate ground, but to develop tolerance for groundlessness while still functioning coherently.
